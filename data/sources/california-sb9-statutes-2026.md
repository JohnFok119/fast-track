## Scope
D8 - SB 9 statutory framework: two-unit housing development under Section 65852.21 and urban lot split under Section 66411.7.

## Verified short excerpts from accessible mirrors

### Government Code Section 65852.21
> "A proposed housing development containing no more than two residential units within a single-family residential zone shall be considered ministerially, without discretionary review or a hearing" (subdivision (a), opening clause; FindLaw).

- Applies only when the statute's eligibility conditions are satisfied.
- See the linked text for all exclusions, objective standards, demolition restrictions, and safety findings; do not treat this short excerpt as the full statute.

### Government Code Section 66411.7
> "a local agency shall ministerially approve, as set forth in this section, a parcel map for an urban lot split" (subdivision (a), excerpt; Justia).
> "one parcel shall not be smaller than 40 percent of the lot area of the original parcel proposed for subdivision" (subdivision (a)(1), excerpt; Justia).
> "both newly created parcels are no smaller than 1,200 square feet" (subdivision (a)(2)(A), excerpt; Justia).
> "would result in a unit size of less than 800 square feet" (subdivision (c)(2), excerpt; Justia).

- Subdivision (a)(2)(B): a local ordinance may allow a smaller minimum parcel size.
- Subdivision (c): local objective standards are permitted subject to statutory limitations.
- Subdivision (d): written, evidence-based public-health-and-safety findings may support denial in the circumstances specified by the statute.
- Subdivision (g): includes an owner-occupancy affidavit requirement and exceptions. Confirm the exact operative 2026 wording before using as a decision rule.
- Subdivision (j): addresses the number of units a local agency must allow on a parcel created by an urban lot split.

## Project use
- Section 65852.21: assess whether a proposed two-unit housing development is eligible for ministerial consideration.
- Section 66411.7: assess urban lot split eligibility, parcel sizing, objective standards, and statutory exceptions.
